# Cursor.com public content layout catalogue

Crawled: **2026-08-03**  
Scope: public pages hosted on `cursor.com`; external properties such as `forum.cursor.com` are excluded.  
Coverage: **2,652 sitemap-listed URLs** across the marketing/content, documentation, and marketplace sitemaps.

This is a layout inventory, not a page-by-page copy inventory. Repeated pages are grouped into reusable templates and section patterns, with representative URLs for each.

## Coverage summary

| Surface | URLs | Included route families |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Marketing and editorial | 158 | Main marketing pages, 98 blog pages, 16 workflow pages, legal pages, careers, community, and events |
| Documentation and learning | 279 | 167 `/docs` pages, 14 `/learn` pages, 19 `/for` pages, and 79 `/help` pages |
| Marketplace | 2,215 | Marketplace index plus plugin, agent, automation, skill, and organization entries |
| **Total** | **2,652** | All URLs listed by Cursor's three `cursor.com` sitemaps at crawl time |

## Page-template layouts

### 1. Flagship narrative homepage

**Example:** [Home](https://cursor.com/)

A long, high-production editorial journey:

1. Large positioning hero with primary conversion action
2. Trust/logo strip
3. Product demonstration modules
4. Alternating feature narratives and interface media
5. Research/frontier positioning
6. Changelog/editorial cards
7. Company mission statement and final CTA

This is the broadest template. It mixes product explanation, social proof, interface demonstrations, editorial content, and conversion sections rather than following one repeated grid.

### 2. Product overview and platform narrative

**Examples:** [Product](https://cursor.com/product), [Enterprise](https://cursor.com/enterprise), [Teams](https://cursor.com/business/teams)

A long-form product story built from modular bands:

- Statement hero
- Logo/customer proof
- Product surface switcher or capability overview
- Alternating text-and-media features
- Multi-card capability groups
- Security, control, or support proof
- Customer stories or testimonials
- Final CTA

`/product` organizes the story around surfaces and the development lifecycle. Enterprise and Teams reuse the narrative shape but add governance, security, support, and organization-level comparisons.

### 3. Focused feature landing page

**Examples:** [Tab](https://cursor.com/tab), [Bugbot](https://cursor.com/bugbot), [CLI](https://cursor.com/cli), [Cloud Agents](https://cursor.com/cloud), [Mobile](https://cursor.com/mobile), [SDK](https://cursor.com/sdk), [Automations](https://cursor.com/automate)

A narrower conversion page for one product capability:

1. Short product-specific hero
2. Product visual or interaction demo
3. Three-to-six benefit sections
4. Supporting cards, examples, or integration surfaces
5. Optional recent highlights, resources, or FAQ
6. Product-specific CTA

Variations:

- **Code-led:** Tab and CLI use code/terminal demonstrations.
- **Interface-led:** Cloud and Mobile use device or workflow visuals.
- **Trust-led:** Bugbot leads with adoption proof and adds FAQ.
- **Developer-platform-led:** SDK adds sample-project cards.
- **Workflow-led:** Automations uses use-case rows spanning the SDLC.

### 4. Model/product launch hub

**Examples:** [Composer](https://cursor.com/composer), [Grok](https://cursor.com/grok)

A product profile that combines launch content with evidence:

- Product-name hero
- Announcement/article cards
- Use-case grid
- Benchmark tables or charts
- User evidence or testimonials
- Model/base-technology explanation
- FAQ and CTA

This template is more editorial and benchmark-heavy than a standard feature landing page.

### 5. Plan and pricing comparison

**Examples:** [Pricing](https://cursor.com/pricing), [Cursor Start](https://cursor.com/start)

- Compact title hero
- Plan cards or tier comparison
- Feature/limit comparison
- Trust strip
- FAQ accordion
- Closing CTA

`/start` blends a product landing page with a smaller pricing comparison before its FAQ.

### 6. Download and installation utility

**Example:** [Download](https://cursor.com/download)

A task-oriented layout rather than a marketing narrative:

- Surface selector for Desktop, Terminal, and Web
- Platform-specific download controls
- Version history grouped by release
- OS cards for macOS, Windows, and Linux
- Installation commands/code blocks where relevant

### 7. Benchmark and interactive evidence pages

**Examples:** [CursorBench](https://cursor.com/cursorbench), [Evals](https://cursor.com/evals), [Cost Savings](https://cursor.com/cost-savings), [Insights](https://cursor.com/insights)

There are three related but distinct templates:

- **Benchmark dashboard:** headline, methodology/context, tabular results, and dated benchmark changelog.
- **Interactive calculator:** proposition hero, input/control panel, calculated result, and benchmark evidence.
- **Research report:** editorial masthead followed by anchored chapters, repeated chart/table modules, findings, and methodology-style prose.

### 8. Customer directory

**Example:** [Customers](https://cursor.com/customers)

- Editorial hero
- Large logo/customer-card wall
- Featured customer stories
- Team CTA

Individual customer stories generally use the blog article template rather than a separate `/customers/:slug` shell.

### 9. Blog hub

**Example:** [Blog](https://cursor.com/blog)

A mixed editorial homepage:

- Blog masthead and topic navigation
- Featured/latest story treatment
- Repeating post-card grids
- Dedicated customer-story row
- Press row
- Video row
- Changelog preview
- Final CTA

### 10. Topic archive

**Examples:** [Product posts](https://cursor.com/blog/topic/product), [Research posts](https://cursor.com/blog/topic/research), [Customer posts](https://cursor.com/blog/topic/customers), [Press posts](https://cursor.com/blog/topic/press)

A simpler index shell:

- Topic title
- Uniform article-card list/grid
- Pagination or finite archive
- Final CTA

The template is shared across topics; card media density varies by category.

### 11. Editorial article

**Examples:** [Composer 2 technical report](https://cursor.com/blog/composer-2-technical-report), [PayPal customer story](https://cursor.com/blog/paypal), [Cursor 3 announcement](https://cursor.com/blog/cursor-3), [Series D company essay](https://cursor.com/blog/series-d)

Shared shell:

1. Category/date metadata and large title
2. Optional dek, author, or hero media
3. Narrow rich-text article column
4. Optional sticky/table-of-contents navigation
5. Inline images, diagrams, video, code, or pull quotes
6. Related-post cards or customer-story cards
7. Global CTA/footer

Content variants:

- **Technical/research report:** deep hierarchy, table of contents, charts/diagrams, methodology, and results.
- **Product announcement:** feature subsections with product media.
- **Customer story:** company framing, outcomes, quotes, and “More customer stories.”
- **Press/company note:** shorter prose, lighter hierarchy, and related posts.

### 12. Changelog feed

**Example:** [Changelog](https://cursor.com/changelog)

A reverse-chronological release stream. Each entry behaves like an embedded article with date/version metadata, a large release title, rich media, nested feature headings, improvement lists, and code-like labels. Multiple releases appear on one continuous page.

### 13. Careers and job directory

**Example:** [Careers](https://cursor.com/careers)

- Mission-led editorial opening
- Culture/company imagery or values content
- “Open roles” directory
- Roles grouped by function or team
- Repeating compact job rows/cards

The job inventory is the dominant repeated content block.

### 14. Community and event directory

**Examples:** [Community](https://cursor.com/community), [Workshops](https://cursor.com/workshops), [Ambassadors](https://cursor.com/ambassadors), [Students](https://cursor.com/students)

Related community templates include:

- **Event directory:** program hero, format cards, events grouped by date, event cards, and location imagery.
- **Workshop library:** upcoming events followed by an on-demand session card grid.
- **Program landing page:** program hero, participant/location gallery, benefits, FAQ, and application CTA.
- **Audience landing page:** social proof, use stories, campus/program CTA, and FAQ.

### 15. Form-led conversion or reporting page

**Examples:** [Contact Sales](https://cursor.com/contact-sales), [Report Abuse](https://cursor.com/abuse)

- **Sales form:** split layout with enterprise proposition and trust marks on one side, structured lead form on the other.
- **Report form:** minimal title, explanatory copy, and a focused submission form.

### 16. Long-form policy, legal, and security document

**Examples:** [Privacy](https://cursor.com/privacy), [Security](https://cursor.com/security), [Terms of Service](https://cursor.com/terms-of-service), [Cookie Policy](https://cursor.com/cookie-policy), [Licenses](https://cursor.com/licenses)

A document-reader shell:

- Plain title and update metadata
- Optional table of contents
- Narrow prose column with anchored section headings
- Occasional lists, tables, badges, or linked disclosures
- Minimal conversion content

Contracts under `/terms/*`, data-use pages, license notices, and cookie tables use the same general long-form shell with differences in navigation depth and tabular content.

### 17. Brand asset guide

- Brand-guidelines title

This is a visual reference and download layout, distinct from both marketing and prose-heavy legal pages.

### 18. Editorial manifesto

### 19. Workflow collection landing page

### 20. Workflow recipe detail

**Examples:** [Find vulnerabilities](https://cursor.com/workflows/autonomous-agents/find-vulnerabilities), [CI autofix](https://cursor.com/workflows/autonomous-agents/ci-autofix)

A repeatable how-to recipe:

1. Recipe title and outcome
2. Introductory “how to” section
3. Prerequisites and integrations
4. Copyable prompt/code block
5. Setup steps
6. Customization tips
7. Table of contents
8. Related automation cards

All detail pages in the workflow family use this shell with different integration badges, prompts, and setup content.

### 21. Documentation landing page

**Example:** [Docs](https://cursor.com/docs)

A documentation-shell landing page with search/navigation in the shared header, a centered introduction, a wide product screenshot with preview behavior, and a responsive “Start here” discovery-card grid.

### 22. Documentation reference article

**Example:** [Agent overview](https://cursor.com/docs/agent/overview)

A documentation reader shell:

- Hierarchical product navigation with nested sections
- Readable central article with title, lead, H2/H3 hierarchy, prose, steps, lists, keyboard-key chips, links, and occasional media
- Secondary article navigation where needed

Reference pages favor dense technical content and deep navigation over marketing CTAs.

### 23. Guided learning lesson

**Example:** [Agents lesson](https://cursor.com/learn/agents)

A course-oriented variant of the docs shell:

- Lightweight lesson/course navigation above a narrower reading column
- Lesson title and conceptual H2 sequence
- Narrative prose with examples and occasional learning/checkpoint modules
- Sequential progression through the learning path

It is more linear and pedagogical than reference documentation.

### 24. Use-case guide

**Examples:** [Cloud Agents setup](https://cursor.com/for/cloud-agents), [Code modernization](https://cursor.com/for/code-modernization), [Code review](https://cursor.com/for/code-review)

A goal-oriented docs article. It keeps the documentation reader shell but organizes content around a job, role, language, or workflow, typically with prerequisites, steps, prompts, examples, and related guides.

### 25. Help-center article

**Example:** [Billing and payments](https://cursor.com/help/account-and-billing/billing)

A support-focused docs variant with a simple “Back to Help” path, concise task/problem title, question-form sections, direct answers, procedural steps, related help links, feedback controls, and support escalation where needed. Articles are generally shorter and more transactional than `/docs` reference pages.

### 26. Marketplace directory

**Example:** [Marketplace](https://cursor.com/marketplace)

A discovery-heavy catalogue inside the marketing shell:

- Marketplace navigation rail/tabs for Featured, Recently Added, Plugins, and Automations
- Utility links for managing automations, documentation, and publishing
- Featured plugin cards
- Featured automation cards
- Recently added row
- Category shelves such as Infrastructure, Data & Analytics, Productivity, and Payments
- Large all-items directory

The index is image-dense and repeats horizontal shelves plus card grids.

### 27. Marketplace item detail

**Example:** [Checkmarx](https://cursor.com/marketplace/checkmarx)

A product-listing detail shell:

- Marketplace breadcrumb/back control
- Icon, title, publisher, category, and dominant install/sign-in action
- Description and capability content
- Creator/source metadata, install slug or command, and capability chips
- Optional setup or configuration instructions
- Related plugins row

Organization-scoped items, agents, automations, and skills reuse this detail structure with type-specific metadata.

## Cross-site composition rules

Across the public site, Cursor repeatedly uses these structural ideas:

1. **One dominant idea per band.** Long pages are split into visually independent full-width sections rather than dense continuous prose.
2. **Evidence follows assertion.** Product claims are commonly followed by UI media, customer logos, benchmarks, or testimonial content.
3. **Editorial and product content share one system.** Blog cards, announcement cards, changelog entries, and product resources use compatible typography and spacing.
4. **Complexity changes by intent.** Marketing pages use wide, media-rich compositions; articles use a narrow reader; docs add persistent navigation; directories prioritize repeated cards or rows.
5. **Most page families end in continuation.** Conversion pages end in a CTA, articles in related content, docs in previous/next links, and directories in more categories/items.

## Notes and limitations

- “All public pages” here means all `cursor.com` URLs advertised in Cursor's public sitemaps on the crawl date. Public but unlisted pages may exist.
- Localized or authenticated dashboard routes were not treated as public content-layout families.
- The 2,215 marketplace URLs are intentionally represented by their shared directory/detail templates instead of one entry per item.
- Content and layouts can change independently of URL structure; recrawl before using this as a pixel-accurate implementation specification.
