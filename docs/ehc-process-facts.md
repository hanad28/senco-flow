## Process map — end to end

### Stage 1 — Request

Who can trigger: parent/carer, young person (16–25), or the school/college.

Legal test (CFA 2014 s.36(8)): does the child have or may have SEN, AND may they need provision through an EHC plan? If yes to both, the LA **must** assess. The same legal test applies regardless of who makes the request — a school-initiated request carries no more legal weight than a parent-initiated one.

LA must reply within **6 weeks** on whether it will assess (SEND Regs 2014, reg.5(1)).

### Stage 2 — Assessment

The LA runs the assessment itself — it cannot delegate this to the school, and cannot make the school pay for any part of it (e.g. an educational psychologist's report).

LA must gather advice from (SEND Regs 2014, reg.6(1)):

- (a) the parent / young person
- (b) educational advice — usually the head teacher
- (c) medical advice from a health care professional
- (d) psychological advice from an educational psychologist
- (e) social care advice
- (f) advice from any other person the LA thinks appropriate
- (g) preparation-for-adulthood advice, if in/beyond Year 9
- (h) advice from anyone the family reasonably requests (e.g. SLT, OT, CAMHS)

Everyone asked for advice — including local NHS services — must respond within **6 weeks** (SEND Regs 2014, reg.8(1); Code para 9.52).

### Stage 3 — Decision

- If no plan: LA must tell the family within **16 weeks** of the original request (SEND Regs 2014, reg.10(1)).
- If a plan is needed: LA must issue the **final plan within 20 weeks** of the original request (SEND Regs 2014, reg.13(2)).
- Before the final plan, LA sends a **draft plan** — family gets **at least 15 days** to comment, request a specific school be named, or request a meeting (draft must not yet name a school — CFA 2014 s.38(5)).
- Staff shortages / waiting lists are explicitly **not** a valid reason to miss any of these deadlines.

### Stage 4 — Naming a school (separate 15-day clock)

If the family requests a specific school (of a type listed in CFA 2014 s.38(3)), the LA must consult that school and give it **15 days** to respond (Code para 9.83; confirmed by IPSEA, Essex SEND IASS, and LGO decision 23 004 539 — always described as "15 days"/"calendar days," never "working days").

**This is a different clock from the family's 15-day comment window** — one runs to the family, one runs to the school. LA can only refuse the family's chosen school if it's unsuitable for the child, or incompatible with efficient education of others / efficient use of resources (CFA 2014 s.39(4)).

### Stage 5 — Annual review (ongoing, 12-month cycle)

- First review within 12 months of the plan being finalised; every 12 months after.
- LA must consult the family and the school/institution, taking account of views, wishes and feelings.
- Review meeting must take place; info from family + professionals circulated 2 weeks before.
- Report written after the meeting, sent to everyone involved.
- LA notifies its decision within **4 weeks** of the meeting.

### Stage 6 — Phase transfer (fixed calendar date, not a rolling clock)

Applies when a child is within 12 months of moving between education phases (early years→school, infant→junior, primary→middle, primary→secondary, middle→secondary, secondary→post-16). Unlike every other deadline above, this is anchored to a **fixed date in the calendar year**, not a rolling window from a trigger event (SEND Regs 2014, reg.18; confirmed directly on IPSEA's dedicated phase-transfer-reviews page):

- **31 March** in the calendar year of transfer, for transfers from secondary school to a post-16 institution.
- **15 February** in the calendar year of transfer, for every other phase transfer.

The LA must review and amend the plan (where necessary) by that date, and the plan must name the setting the child will attend after the transfer. If the review results in proposed amendments, the family still gets the standard **at least 15 days** to respond, and the LA then has a further **8 weeks** from the date of the proposed amendments to issue the final amended plan (or decide not to proceed). A child with an EHC plan should **not** go through the normal school admissions round — they request their preferred setting through this process instead, and are not disadvantaged for skipping the normal process. The fixed date exists specifically so families have time to appeal the placement before the September start.

### Stage 7 — Disagreement & escalation (mediation + SEND Tribunal)

The family can challenge: a refusal to assess, a refusal to issue a plan, the contents of sections B/F/I, the school named, or a decision to cease ("cease to maintain") a plan. Route: mediation first (except for exempt categories — confirm per decision type before building an automated router), then the SEND Tribunal.

**Appeal deadline: 2 months from the date of the decision letter, or 1 month from the date of the mediation certificate — whichever is later.** Confirmed as uniform across appeal types (refusal to assess, refusal to issue a plan, content/placement, cease-to-maintain) via IPSEA's own cease-to-maintain page plus multiple independent SENDIASS sources.

**Exceptions/nuances found on the 1 Aug audit pass:**

- Sections **A** (views/aspirations), **E** (outcomes), and **J** (personal budget) of an EHC plan **cannot** be appealed to the Tribunal.
- There is **no right to appeal a refusal to assess** if the LA has already carried out an EHC needs assessment within the previous 6 months.
- During a **cease-to-maintain** appeal specifically, the existing provision must stay in place and the setting must continue to admit the child until either the appeal deadline passes unused, or the appeal is heard and decided.
- Mediation itself has a working deadline: if not resolved within **30 days**, a certificate is issued, which then opens the 1-month appeal window.

## By party — obligations at a glance

**Family**: requests assessment in writing — no required form or format in law, and an LA cannot reject a request for not using its own hub/portal/template; **must** supply their own written views/evidence as part of the assessment (reg.6(1)(a) — a mandatory input, not an optional add-on); can submit private/independently-commissioned reports, which the LA cannot lawfully refuse to consider; can challenge whether an existing/older report is still "sufficient" to reuse instead of commissioning new advice — this requires the family, the LA, **and** the original author to all agree it's sufficient, so the family alone can force new advice by objecting; can ask the LA to seek advice from a specific *type* of professional under reg.6(1)(h) (e.g. an OT with sensory-processing training — not a named individual); responds to the draft plan within **at least 15 calendar days** (comment/"representations", request a meeting, request a named school); can request a specific school under s.38(3); participates in annual review and gets the same 15-day response right if the LA proposes amendments afterwards; can escalate via mediation then the SEND Tribunal (appeal window: 2 months from decision / 1 month from mediation certificate, whichever later).

**School / SENCO**: gives educational advice during assessment (usually via head teacher); responds to LA's naming consultation within 15 days; delivers day-to-day provision once plan issued; contributes to annual review; often the first port of call before a family even contacts the LA.

**Specialists** (educational psychologist, SLT, OT, health, social care, CAMHS): must respond to LA's advice request within 6 weeks; advice must be independent, not driven by financial/resourcing constraints (Code para 3.19), specific and quantifiable rather than vague (Code para 9.51) — IPSEA explicitly flags wording like "regular," "access to," "as appropriate," "would benefit from" as NOT legally specific enough.

**Local Authority**: owns the whole statutory process — decision to assess (6 wks), gathering advice (6 wks/party), decision on plan (16 wks) or final plan (20 wks), naming consultation (15 days), and annual reviews (12-month cycle). Must issue a plan if the assessment shows provision "won't" be met without one (CFA 2014 s.37(1)).

## Extended stakeholder map (19 Jul 2026)

The core four-party model (Family, School, Specialists, LA) is correct for the day-to-day statutory workflow and right for MVP scope. Additional parties identified below — logged as roadmap awareness, not current build scope.

### Should be modelled as distinct parties as the platform grows

**SENDIASS** (SEN and Disability Information, Advice and Support Service) — every LA is statutorily required to fund one. Independent of the LA; acts on behalf of families who lack capacity/confidence to deal with the LA directly (drafting letters, rights advice, tribunal prep). Distinct from "Family" — lumping it in loses the actual workflow. Also a known distribution channel.

**Designated Clinical Officer / Designated Medical Officer (DCO/DMO)** — sits at the Integrated Care Board (ICB), not the LA. The commissioning-level bridge between the health system and the LA: routes health advice under SEND Regs 2014 reg.6(1)(c), Section G health provision, and health input on SEND Tribunal appeals. Different altitude from individual specialists (SLT/OT/EP who give advice on one child) — the DCO/DMO makes sure that advice translates into funded provision across the system. Not routinely involved in individual assessments (SEND Code of Practice 2015) but the actual point of contact when health provision stalls.

**Social care (children's services)** — technically inside the LA, but a functionally separate team with its own referral process and caseworkers. IPSEA notes LAs commonly get a "not known to this service" response from social care, which does **not** discharge the reg.6(1)(e) duty to seek social care advice. Worth modelling as its own request/response flow rather than folding into a generic "LA" party.

**Post-16 / FE colleges and early years settings** — EHC plans cover ages 0–25. "School" as a party works for compulsory school age but a 17-year-old's plan runs through a college and a 3-year-old's through a nursery/early years setting, each with different named-institution rules (CFA 2014 s.38(3)). Recommendation: model the party as "educational setting" generically rather than "school" literally, to future-proof without extra build cost now.

### Adjacent, not a workflow party

- **SEND Tribunal / mediation services** — dispute-resolution layer, relevant only once something has already gone wrong. Link out to; don't model as a workflow participant.
- **Virtual School Head** — statutory role for looked-after children specifically with SEND. Real, narrow subset of the user base, not core MVP.
- **Parent Carer Forums** — statutory co-production body per LA. More a distribution/partnership channel than a party inside the day-to-day workflow.

### Pitch/build framing

Keep MVP scoped to the four core parties. Add SENDIASS and the DCO/health-commissioning layer to the architecture diagram as "parties 5 and 6" the platform is designed to extend to — signals systems understanding without overscoping the build.

## Source-quality notes (1 Aug 2026) — inaccuracies in secondary sources, do not propagate

Cross-checked two non-authoritative parent-facing guides against IPSEA/legislation while building the family-side facts base. Both are fine for general parent orientation but contain inaccuracies that must never leak into product copy or deadline logic:

- **A Twinkl parent guide** invents a "12-week deadline for draft [plan]" — there is no such statutory deadline. IPSEA's guidance is that the draft *should* be issued by week 14 as a practical target to leave room for the 15-day family response before the week-20 final-plan deadline, but this is not a legal cap and must never be presented as one.
- The same guide frames evidence-gathering as a fixed "weeks 6–12" window — the actual rule is relative (each professional has 6 weeks from when *they're* asked, not from week 6 of the overall timeline). A fixed-window framing risks telling a family to chase someone at the wrong time.
- **gov.uk's own SEND guide** compresses the 6-week decision-to-assess and the 16-week decision-to-issue-a-plan into a single "we'll tell you within 16 weeks" statement. Not wrong, but loses a distinction families need to know when to first escalate a stalled request.
- Primary legislation (SEND Regs 2014, reg.13(1)) confirms the draft-plan response window verbatim as "at least 15 days... beginning with the day on which the draft plan was served" — no "working days" qualifier, which under standard UK drafting convention defaults to calendar days. This matches the calendar-day correction already made on the SENDCO side and should be treated as settled.

## Additional family-facing rights & mechanics (1 Aug 2026 audit pass)

Found while stress-testing the family-side breakdown for completeness — genuinely required by the facts, not previously logged:

- **Unlawful extra criteria**: an LA cannot lawfully refuse an assessment request for lack of things like proof of a specific prior spend (commonly £6,000/year), a specific percentile/years-behind threshold, a minimum number of terms of SEN support, or prior involvement from an outside specialist. None of these are part of the legal test (CFA 2014 s.36(8): has/may have SEN + may need EHC-plan provision). Families should be able to recognise when an LA is applying one of these unlawfully.
- **Draft plan must not name a placement**: a draft EHC plan naming a school/college/setting, or even a type of setting, at draft stage is itself a compliance problem — need→provision→placement must happen in that order (CFA 2014 s.38/reg.13; IPSEA).
- **Right to participation support (reg.9)**: the LA must consider whether the family needs information, advice or support to fully take part in the assessment, and must provide it if so.
- **"Reasonableness" test for requesting a specific professional (reg.6(1)(h))**: a request is more likely to count as reasonable where the child is already on a waiting list for that type of professional, or another professional/the school has already said that advice may be needed — worth reflecting in how such a request gets framed.
- **Grounds for refusing a family's preferred school** are limited to three: unsuitable for the child's age/ability/skills/SEN; incompatible with the efficient education of others; or an inefficient use of the LA's resources (CFA 2014 s.39(4)) — a family can check any refusal against this closed list.
- **Consultation mechanic**: when a family requests a specific school, the LA must consult that institution before deciding whether to name it — with different rules for a wholly independent school.
